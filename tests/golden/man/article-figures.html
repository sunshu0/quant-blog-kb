Valuation Spreads in a Toy Universe

7 May 2024

Fixture notice: this page is for automated tests only and is not investment material.

We build a tiny pretend market to see how wide the gap between cheap and dear stocks can grow.

Setting the scene

Imagine a market with only twelve listed firms, each sorted by a single price ratio.1 The cheapest third forms one bucket and the dearest third forms another.

What moved it

Most of the widening came from the dear bucket getting dearer, not from the cheap bucket getting cheaper.2

We also tried splitting the dear bucket in two. The top sliver did nearly all of the work, which is what you would expect when one or two pretend firms are priced for a miracle and the rest are merely optimistic.

Conclusion

A toy universe cannot tell us what real markets will do, but it is a cheap way to check that a pipeline keeps headings, figures and notes in order.
